# Privacy Policy

Version 1, effective 2026-11-04.

> **In short (not the binding text; the numbered sections below are):**
> We keep what GitHub tells us when you sign in, what you accept, what you publish and report, and a log of who did what. That is it.
> We keep no IP address, except for two days inside a counter that stops floods.
> One cookie keeps you signed in (and a short one lives for minutes during sign-in). No analytics, no ads, no tracking pixels, no selling, no sharing for ads. Ever.
> Two companies run the machines for us: Cloudflare and GitHub.
> You can see, fix, take away or delete your data. Write to julia@wolos.org; we answer within 15 days.
> A few things stay even after you leave: the public log, the revocation list, and what we need to answer a court or defend ourselves. The law lets us keep those, and we keep nothing more.
> If someone's life is at risk, or a crime is on the table, we talk to the police.
> 16 or older only: you confirm it when you accept the Terms of Use; we do not check ages.

## 2. What we never do

- We never sell your personal data, and we never share it for advertising.
- We show no advertising and use no analytics, tracking pixels, fingerprinting or third-party scripts.
- We never ask for your password, your signing keys, your ID documents, or any sensitive data (health, beliefs, ethnicity, sex life, biometrics, precise location).
- We never build profiles of you, and we never make decisions about you with legal effect by machine alone.

## 3. What we keep

We keep exactly these categories. Nothing else.

3.1. **Your GitHub account**, as GitHub gives it to us when you sign in: your numeric GitHub id, login, display name and avatar address. With them, two facts GitHub reports about the account: when it was created, and whether two-factor sign-in is on (and when we last checked). We add what the hub itself needs to run your account: your role on the hub (member, moderator or certificate operator), when you first signed in and when you last signed in, when you last signed out (every older session stops working), and whether the account is banned.

3.2. **Your organisation memberships**: the GitHub organisations GitHub says you belong to, and your role in them.

3.3. **Your acceptances**: which texts you accepted (Contributor Licence Agreement, Terms of Use, Publisher Agreement, Trust Terms), which version, the hash of the exact text, and the time.

3.4. **Listings and releases**: the metadata of what you publish: names, summaries, licences, versions, repository addresses, release addresses, file hashes, sizes, icons and screenshots, and the hub's check results.

3.5. **Reports**: reports you send (the listing, the reason, your text) and the moderator's decision.

3.6. **Certificate requests and Certificates**: your request (subject, level, public key, the rules checked at the time and their values, such as account age and two-factor status), the decision, and the public Certificate.

3.7. **Audit log**: a record of each action that changes something on the hub: who did it (GitHub id or system), what, on what, when, the GitHub login at that time, and, for actions in the admin panel, an address class: whether the connection was IPv4 or IPv6 and the country it came from. **We do not store your IP address in the audit log.**

3.8. **Webhook delivery ids**: the ids GitHub gives to the messages it sends us about repositories, so we do not handle the same message twice.

3.9. **Rate counters**: counts of requests per minute or per day, per account or per network address, used to stop floods and abuse. A counter's key contains the network address only for the per-address limits. **This is the only place where the hub's database stores an IP address, and only for at most 2 days.** The machines that run the hub (section 6.3) keep their own request logs, which we do not control.

3.10. **Session cookie**: one cookie in your browser, described in section 12 and in the [Cookie Notice](/terms/cookies). It holds your GitHub id and login and your GitHub sign-in token, sealed with encryption so only the hub can read it. The hub keeps no copy of the token.

3.11. **Messages you send us** by email (for example a privacy request or a notice), with your email address. We keep them only to answer and to show we answered.

## 4. Why we use it, and on what legal basis

GDPR article 6(1) and LGPD article 7 require a legal basis for each purpose. We use no consent-based processing, so there is nothing to withdraw; you can object instead (section 8).

- **Sign you in and keep you signed in**. Data: 3.1, 3.10. GDPR: 6(1)(b) contract. LGPD: 7 V contract.
- **Act for organisations you belong to**. Data: 3.2. GDPR: 6(1)(b) contract. LGPD: 7 V contract.
- **Prove what you accepted and when**. Data: 3.3. GDPR: 6(1)(f) legitimate interest (proving the agreement); 6(1)(c) where a law asks. LGPD: 7 VI exercise of rights; 7 IX legitimate interest.
- **List, check and publish your Work; sign the index**. Data: 3.4. GDPR: 6(1)(b) contract. LGPD: 7 V contract.
- **Show who published what and keep the public log**. Data: 3.4, 3.6, 3.7. GDPR: 6(1)(f) legitimate interest (people can check what they install). LGPD: 7 IX legitimate interest.
- **Handle reports and moderate**. Data: 3.5, 3.7. GDPR: 6(1)(f) legitimate interest (a hub without abuse); 6(1)(c) legal obligation for notices under the EU Digital Services Act. LGPD: 7 IX legitimate interest; 7 II legal obligation.
- **Issue, review and revoke Certificates**. Data: 3.1, 3.6. GDPR: 6(1)(b) contract; 6(1)(f) legitimate interest (stopping misuse). LGPD: 7 V contract; 7 IX legitimate interest.
- **Stop floods, abuse and attacks**. Data: 3.7, 3.8, 3.9. GDPR: 6(1)(f) legitimate interest (keeping the hub running). LGPD: 7 IX legitimate interest.
- **Answer your messages and requests**. Data: 3.11. GDPR: 6(1)(c) legal obligation; 6(1)(f). LGPD: 7 II legal obligation; 7 IX.
- **Defend or bring legal claims, answer authorities**. Data: any, as needed. GDPR: 6(1)(c), 6(1)(f). LGPD: 7 II, 7 VI.
- **Protect someone's life or physical integrity, report a suspected crime**. Data: any, as needed. GDPR: 6(1)(d) vital interests; 6(1)(c) where a law asks (Digital Services Act, article 18); 6(1)(f). LGPD: 7 VII protection of life; 7 II; 7 IX.

4.1. Our legitimate interests: running a public catalogue that people can check, stopping malware and impersonation, keeping the hub available, and proving what was agreed. We weighed them against your rights: the data is small, mostly public already on GitHub, and you expect it to be used this way.

4.2. Automatic rules (two-factor, account age, rate limits, checks) decide whether a request can go ahead. They do not have legal effect on you; a person reviews any of them if you ask (Terms of Use, section 11). This also answers LGPD article 20.

## 5. How long we keep it

- **GitHub account (3.1), memberships (3.2)**: while you have a hub account. Memberships are refreshed at each sign-in. Deleted within 30 days of your deletion request.
- **Acceptances (3.3)**: while you have a hub account, then 5 years, to prove the agreement in a dispute.
- **Listings and releases (3.4)**: while the listing exists. What entered the signed index and the public log stays there, because people use it to check what they installed.
- **Reports (3.5)**. How long: 2 years after the decision, then deleted automatically. A report nobody has decided yet stays until it is.
- **Certificate requests (3.6)**. How long: 5 years after the decision, then deleted automatically; where the request became a public Certificate, what you wrote in it is emptied and the Certificate itself stays. Public Certificates and revocations stay in the public record for as long as it exists.
- **Audit log (3.7)**: as long as the hub runs. When your account is deleted, we remove the login from your entries, so they keep only your numeric GitHub id.
- **Webhook delivery ids (3.8)**. How long: 30 days, deleted automatically.
- **Rate counters (3.9)**. How long: 2 days, deleted automatically.
- **Session cookie (3.10)**. How long: 7 days at most, or until you sign out.
- **Messages to us (3.11)**. How long: 3 years after the last message, then deleted.

5.1. We keep data longer only when a law or a court requires it, or while a dispute about it is open.

## 6. Who sees it

6.1. **The public.** Listings, releases, check results, public Certificates, the revocation list and the public log are public. They show GitHub logins and ids, because that is how people know who published what. **Reports and the names of people who report are never public.**

6.2. **Our moderators and reviewers**, under a duty of confidentiality, only for what their role needs.

6.3. **Our processors**, who run the hub's machines for us under data processing contracts:
- **Cloudflare, Inc.** (United States, and its data centres worldwide): hosting of the pages, the hub's code, its database and key-value store, and delivery of the cookie.
- **GitHub, Inc.** (United States): automated jobs (GitHub Actions) and repositories that hold the signed index, the public log and the revocation list.

6.3.1. **Two more things happen in your browser, and the other party sees them.** (a) The small picture of a signed-in person is loaded from GitHub's image servers (avatars.githubusercontent.com): GitHub sees your network address and that the request came from hub.wolos.org (not which page). (b) Cloudflare asks browsers to send it network-error reports about hub.wolos.org (a browser feature called Network Error Logging) for up to 7 days at a time; the report names the hub address and the kind of failure. Neither is a script, and neither tells us anything.

6.4. **GitHub as its own controller.** When you sign in, GitHub handles your GitHub account under its own privacy statement. We receive only what section 3.1 and 3.2 list.

6.5. **Authorities and courts**: when a law or a valid order requires it, after checking that the request is valid; and, without an order, only where the law allows it: to protect someone's life or physical integrity, to report a suspected crime that threatens them, or to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. We give only what is needed. We tell you, unless the law, the order or the risk forbids it, and then as soon as that reason ends.

6.6. **A successor**, if someone takes over the hub. They must keep this policy or give you notice under section 17.

## 8. Your rights

8.1. Everyone, wherever you live, has these rights with us:
- **Know and access**: confirm that we hold your data and get a copy.
- **Correct**: fix data that is wrong or incomplete. Most of it comes from GitHub: change it there and sign in again.
- **Delete**: ask us to delete your data. We delete it, except what the public log and the revocation list must keep and what a law requires us to keep (section 5).
- **Portability**: get your data in a common machine-readable format.
- **Object** to processing based on legitimate interest (GDPR article 21). We stop unless we have strong reasons that override yours, or need it for legal claims.
- **Restrict**: ask us to pause using data while a question about it is open.
- **Information** about who we share with (section 6), and about the effects of not giving data: without a GitHub account you cannot sign in.
- **Review of automatic decisions** by a person (section 4.2).
- **No discrimination**: using a right never costs you anything and never changes how the hub treats you.

8.2. **In the EU and the EEA**, these rights come from GDPR articles 15 to 22. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live or work (GDPR article 77).

8.3. **In Brazil**, these rights come from LGPD article 18, including anonymisation, blocking and deletion of data that is unnecessary or excessive, and information about sharing. You can also complain to the Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados (ANPD).

8.4. **In California and other US states**, see section 11.

8.5. **Limits the law sets on these rights.** We apply a limit only where the law that protects you allows it, and we tell you which one we rely on:
- we keep data we must keep by law, or need to establish, exercise or defend legal claims (GDPR article 17(3)(b) and (e); LGPD article 16, I; CCPA section 1798.105(d));
- we do not give you data that would reveal another person, such as the name of someone who reported you (GDPR article 15(4); in Brazil, the reporter's own rights under the LGPD);
- we may go on using data after an objection where we have compelling legitimate grounds, or need it for legal claims (GDPR article 21(1));
- we do not need to keep or collect extra data only to identify you for a request (GDPR article 11);
- entries already in the public log and the revocation list stay, because they protect everyone who installed something; after a deletion we reduce them to your numeric GitHub id.

## 9. How to use your rights

9.1. Write to julia@wolos.org from the email linked to your GitHub account, or write from any address and tell us your GitHub login. To confirm it is you, we may ask you to sign in to the hub or to post a short code in a place only your account controls. We never ask for ID documents.

9.2. An authorised agent may act for you, with your signed permission; we may confirm with you directly.

9.3. **Response times:** we confirm right away that we received your request and answer in full **within 15 days**. If a request is complex, we tell you within those 15 days why we need more time, and we never take longer than the law where you live allows (GDPR: 3 months in total; CCPA: 90 days in total).

9.4. Answers are free. If requests are clearly unfounded or excessive, for example because they repeat, we may refuse and explain why, or, where the law allows (GDPR article 12(5); CCPA section 1798.145), charge a reasonable fee. In Brazil, answers are always free (LGPD article 18, §5).

9.5. **If we refuse**, we tell you why. You can ask us to look again (an appeal) by answering our message within 60 days; we answer the appeal within 45 days and tell you how to complain to the authority where you live.

## 11. California and other US states

11.4. **Opt-out signals.** We honour Global Privacy Control and similar opt-out preference signals. Because we never sell or share, the signal changes nothing; we still treat it as a valid opt-out.

11.5. **Do Not Track.** We do not track you across sites or over time on other services, so a Do Not Track signal changes nothing. No other party collects data about your activity on the hub beyond the two things section 6.3.1 names (California Business and Professions Code section 22575).

11.7. **Your rights** under the CCPA (Civil Code sections 1798.100 to 1798.125): to know, access, correct and delete, to opt out of selling and sharing, and not to be discriminated against for using them. We answer within 45 days, extended once by 45 days where needed, with notice. Section 9 explains how.

11.8. **Residents of Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas and other states with privacy laws** have the same rights in practice: access, correction, deletion, portability, opt-out of sale, targeted advertising and profiling (none happens), and the right to appeal a refusal (section 9.5).

11.9. In the last 12 months we disclosed the categories in 11.1 only to the processors in section 6.3, for running the hub.

## 12. Cookies

12.1. The hub sets **one cookie**: the session cookie. It is strictly necessary to keep you signed in, so it needs no consent. It lasts 7 days at most, is sent only to hub.wolos.org over encrypted connections, and cannot be read by scripts on the page (HttpOnly).

12.2. During sign-in, a second short cookie may hold the sign-in state for at most 10 minutes, so the hub can match GitHub's answer to your request. It is deleted when sign-in ends.

12.3. The pages may keep your chosen colour theme in your browser's local storage. It never leaves your device.

12.4. No analytics, advertising or third-party cookies. The [Cookie Notice](/terms/cookies) has the details.

14.2. If we learn that a hub account belongs to someone under 16, we close it and delete its data within 30 days, except what the public log must keep, which we reduce to the numeric GitHub id. Parents or guardians can write to julia@wolos.org.

## 15. How we protect your data

- Connections to the hub are always encrypted.
- Your GitHub sign-in token travels only inside your cookie, encrypted so only the hub can read it, and the cookie is signed so nobody can change it.
- We store no passwords, no signing keys and, apart from rate counters, no IP addresses in the hub's database.
- The admin panel's data is closed: it answers like a missing page to anyone without a role. (The empty shell of its page and its program file are public, and show nothing.) Roles have only the rights they need. Every action is in the audit log. Revoking a Certificate for abuse needs two people.
- The signing keys of the hub's index and of the Certificate authority are not on the hub's web servers.
- We keep the data in section 3 and nothing more, and delete it on time (section 5).
- Our processors are bound by data processing contracts and their own protection programmes.

These measures fit the risk of the small amount of data we hold (GDPR article 32; LGPD article 46). No system is perfect, and no measure can rule out every attack. If something goes wrong, section 16 applies.

## 16. If there is a breach

16.1. If a breach of personal data may put you at risk, we:
- tell the competent data protection authorities within 72 hours of learning of it (GDPR article 33), and the ANPD within 3 working days (LGPD article 48 and ANPD rules);
- tell you within 3 working days, or sooner when a law asks for it, in plain words: what happened, what data, what we did, what you can do;
- follow the notice rules of US states where they apply to you.

16.2. We keep a record of every breach, even small ones.

## 17. Changes to this policy

We change this policy only by a new version, listed in the [Changes Log](/terms/changes). For a change that matters to you, we tell you at least 30 days before it takes effect, on the hub and by GitHub notification or email when we have one. We never start a new use of your data without telling you first and, where the law asks for it, getting your consent.
